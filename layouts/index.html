The whole of PDF in Go,
with no C anywhere.

Read a file, write one, rearrange it, draw it, read it back as words and pictures, and edit it with other people — in a browser tab if you like, since every one of these builds for js/wasm. Fonts are parsed and shaped by go-opentype, pages are rasterised by go-gfx. Pure Go, CGO_ENABLED=0. BSD-3-Clause.

Measured, not asserted

Against 118 863 real PDFs, not a handful written to pass a test.

arXiv's figures, Matplotlib and Mathematica and pdfTeX and Ghostscript and Adobe. That corpus is what found a stroke that came out at half its colour, a font that took the dots off every i, and a colour transform that turned every plot's paper yellow. Each of them is in the release notes of the library it was found in.

📂

They open

118 833 of the 118 835 files that really are PDFs. Of the 118 863 collected, 27 are PNGs named .pdf and one is a PostScript file; of the rest, two are truncated past recovery.

🔁

They round-trip

1 536 769 753 content operations read, and every one of them rewrites to an identical fingerprint. 118 833 files encrypt, decrypt and compress with every page byte for byte the same.

🖌️

They draw

4 108 pages rasterise with no panics and six blank, and the biggest changes are put beside what the operating system's own renderer draws — pixels hashed before and after, never an exit status.

🔤

They read back

121 946 pages come back as 34 million characters, each piece with a page, a place, a size and what it says. A piece the document gave no way to read is marked rather than guessed at.

The API

Verbs on a document, and a page as pixels.

The same operations the command line exposes are a library first. Nothing shells out, nothing is fetched at run time, and the whole of it compiles to one js/wasm module with no SharedArrayBuffer and no CDN.

// Take three pages out of a report, turn one the right way up,
// stamp the lot, and write it out.
doc, _ := ops.Open(bytes)
doc.Select("4-6")
doc.SetRotation("2", 90)
doc.Watermark("all", "DRAFT")
out, _ := doc.Bytes()

🔄

s390x is big-endian

Every lane was green from the first run, which is the useful kind of result: nothing in the stack had quietly assumed a byte order. A PDF is full of two- and four-byte integers, and a reader that reads them the wrong way round on one machine is a reader nobody can trust on any.

📏

386 and arm are 32-bit

They found four real defects. One of them — a guard converting a float before it could judge it — crashes on arm64 as well, so a 32-bit lane paid for itself on a 64-bit machine. 386 runs natively, not under emulation, because an emulator that loses floating-point state across a preemption makes a flaky lane out of correct code.

⚠️

loong64 is pinned, and we say why

Its lane names Go 1.26.4 rather than the current release, because golang/go#81000 miscompiles a float-to-integer conversion there and the backport is still open. We found it with this suite, on real LoongArch silicon, and reported it upstream. A pinned lane with a reason beats a green one with a workaround nobody wrote down.

🌐

and js/wasm, which is the workbench

The same libraries compile to one wasm binary with no C, no CDN and no SharedArrayBuffer — which is why the workbench opens a file, rearranges it and saves it back without any of it leaving the machine.

Text, fonts and widgets

A subset, an encoding, and text you can still copy.

✂️

Every embedded font is a subset

Identity-H encoding, a per-glyph /W width array and a /ToUnicode CMap, so copy and paste still work. TrueType glyf outlines embed as a subsetted FontFile2 / CIDFontType2 with a /CIDToGIDMap stream; CFF and OpenType outlines embed as a charstring-subsetted FontFile3 / CIDFontType0. Only a CID-keyed CFF or a CFF2 variable font falls back to embedding the whole table.

🌍

Shaped, where shaping is the point

TextShaped runs the go-opentype shaper's GSUB and GPOS tables, so Arabic joins, Indic reorders and CJK sits where it should — rather than a run of glyphs laid out left to right and called typesetting.

🧩

A widget tree, printed

Page.AddWidget and Page.AddWidgetVector put a go-widgets/toolkit tree on a page — the first as a rasterised image XObject, pixel-identical to the screen; the second as PDF vector operators, so fills and strokes stay crisp and the text stays selectable, a TrueType widget label's own face embedded as real Type0 text.

🎯

The same bytes twice

The writer is deterministic: the same document written twice is the same file, which is what makes a before-and-after comparison mean anything at all.

100 % statement coverage, including the branches that report a bad file.
